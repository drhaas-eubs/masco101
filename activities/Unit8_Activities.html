MASCO101 · Unit 8 · Session Plan

Hackathon · Capstone: Arclight Consulting

Seven phases, 150 minutes, no lecture

Hackathon · Capstone150 minutes7 phasesSlibraries 15 & 16
Core message · Unit 8

Delegate the task. Never delegate the accountability.

Learning focus · ILOs 1 & 3 — Productivity systems & AI workflow judgment. ILO 1: Systematically select key concepts and principles of operations and technology management that need to be implemented to support value creation. ILO 3: Investigate how to select, develop, plan, schedule and measure projects outcomes and risks.

Learning intention

Students leave able to distinguish energy management from time management in their own week, classify a task on the Task Delegation Matrix before delegating it, set one personal OKR, build a prompt to the five-part anatomy (role, task, context, format, constraints) with a verification step, and write and defend a personal AI charter.

Guiding question: What will you delegate, what will you verify, and what will you never hand over?

Phase by phase

1The Brief & Team Formation10 min

Teams of four, mixed across Assessment 3 groups.

The brief: design a personal productivity and AI workflow that a first-year analyst could adopt on Monday and still be using in six months.

Your task: Agree one real, specific, recurring professional task — for example, a weekly two-page competitor summary for a director who reads it on a phone.

Vague briefs produce vague workflows, and the Red Team will take them apart.

2Gallery Exploration — Slibraries 15 & 1620 min

Ten frameworks: five on energy, time and priority, five on AI workflow and professional judgment.

The Task Delegation Matrix and the Delegate–Verify–Own loop carry the build.

Your task: Plot your chosen task on the Task Delegation Matrix — stakes against verifiability — and state its quadrant.

High stakes and hard to verify means do not delegate. If your task lands there, decompose it or choose another.

3Sprint 1 — Map the Week, Find the Drain25 min

Ten minutes individually, then team synthesis.

Map last week honestly against the Eisenhower Matrix and the four energy dimensions.

Your task: Identify where your time went and where your energy went — they are different maps — and name the largest drain that is not an urgent-and-important activity. Then write one personal OKR for the rest of term: one objective, three measurable key results.

Typical finding: urgent but unimportant work consumes the capacity that important, non-urgent work needed.

4Sprint 2 — Build the AI Workflow30 min

Team build: a working artefact, not a slide about one.

Required: a prompt built to the five-part Prompt Anatomy — Role, Task, Context, Format, Constraints — a verification step calibrated to stakes, a disclosure line, and a named human owner.

Test the prompt twice: once as a one-line request, once with all five parts. Keep both outputs for the Demo Wall.

Your task: Build and test the workflow on the real task. Run it at least twice and record what failed the first time.

A workflow with no verification step will not survive Phase 5 — nor will one whose verification costs more than the time saved.

— Break · 10 min —

5The Red Team25 min

Teams swap workflows and have twenty minutes to break someone else's.

Attack vectors: a hallucinated number; the verification step skipped on a Friday; the builder leaving the organisation.

Your task: Find at least three failure modes, rank them by consequence, and hand back a specific written attack summary.

The Red Team is scored on the damage it finds. Politeness is not the goal here.

6Demo Wall20 min

Five minutes per team: two to demonstrate, one to state the strongest attack, two to show the fix.

Judged on whether it survives the attack and whether a real analyst would still use it in month six.

Your task: Demonstrate the workflow live, name the strongest attack honestly, and show what you changed.

Teams that name the strongest attack and partly fix it score higher than teams that hide it.

7Reflection & Portfolio Close10 min

Individual writing: the final portfolio entry of the taught course.

Your task: Write your personal AI charter in three lines — what you delegate, what you always verify, what you never hand over. Then answer: what is your Human Edge now, and how has that changed since Unit 1?

Compare with your Unit 1 entry. The distance between the two answers is what Assessment 2 measures.

AI in this unit

What we teach

  • The current AI landscape at capability level rather than product level: what these systems do well, badly, and not at all.
  • Prompt literacy against the five-part anatomy: role, task, context, format, constraints.
  • The Task Delegation Matrix, the Delegate–Verify–Own loop, and calibrating verification to consequence.
  • Attribution, academic integrity and the professional consequences of undisclosed use — including why a model cannot be an author.

Practised in session: The full hackathon: build a workflow, have it attacked by a Red Team, and defend the fix on the Demo Wall.

Rule: The workflow you build is yours to keep and use across the remaining MASCO courses. The disclosure line it generates is not optional — it is part of the artefact.

Debrief questions

  1. Which quadrant of the Task Delegation Matrix did most teams' tasks fall into?
  2. What was the most common attack the Red Teams found?
  3. Which verification steps were structural (built into the workflow) rather than instructions?
Link to assessmentAssessment 2 — Human Edge Portfolio: personal AI charter and workflow; the final Human Edge statement.

After the session, students answer the three reflection questions within 48 hours and add them to their Personal Slibrary.
