MASCO101 · Unit 1 · Session Plan

Guided Discovery: Lumen Analytics

Seven phases, 150 minutes, no lecture

Guided Discovery150 minutes7 phasesSlibraries 1 & 2
Core message · Unit 1

AI collapses the cost of execution; it raises the price of judgment.

Learning focus · ILO 1 — Foundations & digital fluency. ILO 1: Systematically select key concepts and principles of operations and technology management that need to be implemented to support value creation.

Learning intention

Students leave able to explain why fluent execution has become cheap and judgment has not, evaluate a source or AI output with SIFT and the CRAAP test, locate a generative output on the Hallucination Risk Ladder, apply the AI Disclosure Ladder to their own work, and open their Human Edge Portfolio with a first-draft Personal SWOT and one goal for the term.

Guiding question: What makes you irreplaceable when the machine can already do the task?

Phase by phase

1The Cold Open10 min

I read a two-paragraph strategy memo aloud. It is competent, well structured and entirely fluent. One of you will be asked whether we should act on it.

Then two minutes of silence: no notes, no discussion, no devices.

Your task: Decide silently whether you would sign the memo. Be ready to say what you would need to know first.

The memo was generated in eleven seconds. The discomfort you feel is the subject of this course: fluency is now free, and judgment is not.

2Course Contract & the Slibrary Method15 min

How MASCO101 works: eight units, sixteen Slibraries, eighty frameworks, three assessments. The gallery stays open in every session and in both assessments.

Thumbnail Thinking (Meier, 2025): you build a mental catalogue of visuals to flip through under pressure. You learn which shape fits which problem; you do not memorise eighty frameworks.

Your task: Open the Unit 1 gallery on your device now and keep it open for the rest of the term.

We agree the AI contract today: what is permitted, what must be disclosed, and what is never delegated. It is binding for all assessments.

3Gallery Exploration — Slibraries 1 & 225 min

Ten frameworks: five on the Human Edge, five on digital and AI literacy. Work in pairs.

Open every card and read the definition and the primary source. You are building a shortlist.

Your task: Select the three frameworks that would have helped you answer the Cold Open question. For each, write the name, the Slibrary number and one sentence: why this one?

Draw from both Slibraries. A judgment lens combined with a verification lens is stronger than three frameworks from the same set.

4Framework Pitch20 min

Four pairs present. Ninety seconds each, strictly timed.

Your task: Name your three frameworks, state the Slibrary each comes from, and explain in one sentence why that combination.

Expect challenge: you chose two verification frameworks and no judgment framework — what happens when the source checks out and the recommendation is still wrong?

— Break · 10 min —

5Case Deep Dive: Lumen Analytics35 min

Teams of four. Five minutes to read, thirty to build.

You are the engagement partner. An analyst submitted a client deliverable containing a fabricated citation. The client has already seen it.

Your task: Produce a one-page visual argument — sketches and arrows, not paragraphs — covering what went wrong at process level, which framework explains it, and the verification rule you will impose tomorrow morning.

The best answers resist blaming the analyst. Look for the process that made the failure predictable.

6The Verification Race — SIFT25 min

Every team receives the same three claims. One is true and well sourced, one is true but badly sourced, one is fabricated but plausible.

Fifteen minutes. Lateral reading only: leave the page to check the source.

Your task: Classify all three claims and state the single SIFT move that settled each one. Speed and accuracy both count.

The claim that fools most teams is usually the one with a real institution attached to an invented number.

7Reflection, Personal SWOT & Personal Slibrary10 min

Individually, no discussion.

Open your Personal Slibrary — the running document you maintain all term and the raw material of Assessment 2. It starts with a first-draft Personal SWOT: two honest entries per cell, strengths and weaknesses from inside, opportunities and threats from the AI-shaped job market.

Your task: Write your first-draft Personal SWOT and one goal for the term. Then add three entries: one framework from today you will actually use, one question you are still holding, and one sentence on what your Human Edge currently is.

The SWOT is deliberately rough. In Unit 2 you evidence every cell and turn it into TOWS actions and a SMART roadmap.

AI in this unit

What we teach

  • How generative models produce fabricated but well-formed citations, and why the format looks more reliable than the content.
  • Lateral reading (SIFT) as a faster and more reliable verification method than reading the page more carefully.
  • The AI Disclosure Ladder: five levels of declared use, applied to every assessment in this course.

Practised in session: The Verification Race in Phase 6: three claims, fifteen minutes, lateral reading only.

Rule: From this session onwards, every submitted artefact in MASCO101 carries a disclosure line stating your level on the AI Disclosure Ladder. An accurate Level 3 declaration is never penalised. An inaccurate Level 0 declaration is an integrity matter.

Debrief questions

  1. Where exactly did the Lumen workflow fail — at the analyst, the review, or the absence of a rule?
  2. Which SIFT move settled the fabricated claim fastest, and why did the format of the claim look trustworthy?
  3. What is the difference between checking the substance of a claim and checking that its source exists?

After the session, students answer the three reflection questions within 48 hours and add them to their Personal Slibrary.
