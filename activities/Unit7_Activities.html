Role Play — Stakeholder Negotiation: Verdana Foods

Seven phases, 150 minutes, no lecture

Role Play — Stakeholder Negotiation150 minutes7 phasesSlibraries 13 & 14
Core message · Unit 7

Power in a negotiation is not what you hold. It is what you can walk to.

Learning focus · ILO 4 — Negotiation, influence & ethical boundaries. ILO 4: Evaluate the keys to successful negotiation and differentiate negotiation styles, strategies, and tactics.

Learning intention

Students leave able to estimate their own and the counterparty's BATNA before entering the room, complete a 5W1H Negotiation Canvas with a written reservation point, create joint value by trading across issues of differing priority, influence without authority using Cohen and Bradford's currencies, and locate a tactic on the ethical line between persuasion and deception.

Guiding question: What do you have that they want, when you have no title at all?

Phase by phase

1Boostcamp Wrap-up15 min

The Boostcamp episode on power, influence and ethics was completed before this session.

We close it and move straight into preparation.

Your task: Name the one tactic from the episode you would not use, and say why.

Keep your answer. We return to it in Phase 6, and your position may move.

2Gallery Exploration — Slibraries 13 & 1420 min

Ten frameworks: five on negotiation architecture, five on influence, power and ethics.

BATNA, ZOPA, the Harvard Method and the Negotiation Canvas are the working instruments.

Your task: In pairs, complete the Influence Currencies worksheet for the Verdana scenario: list the currencies you can offer Norlund and those Norlund values most. You have no formal authority.

Information, expertise, commitment and recognition are currencies anyone can trade. That is the point of this unit.

3The Negotiation Canvas — 5W1H25 min

Individually, then in role pairs. One page, six sections.

Who, What, Why, Where, When, How: parties and mandates, issues and priorities, underlying interests, setting, sequencing and process.

Your task: Complete the canvas for your role. Estimate your BATNA and theirs. Write your reservation point down before you enter the room, and do not move it during Negotiation 1.

A reservation point decided at the table is not a reservation point. It is a mood.

4Negotiation 1 — Distributive25 min

Twenty minutes at the table, five to record. One issue only: unit price.

The single issue makes the fixed-pie dynamic unavoidable.

Your task: Reach a deal or walk away. Record the final number, your reservation point, and whether you moved it.

Some pairs fail to agree even where a ZOPA exists. The debrief usually shows why: nobody asked why.

— Break · 10 min —

5Negotiation 2 — Integrative30 min

Same parties, six issues: price, volume, payment terms, delivery schedule, quality guarantee and contract length.

Twenty-five minutes at the table, five to record.

Your task: Trade across issues. Find at least two trades where something cheap for you is valuable to them, and record the joint value created compared with Negotiation 1.

Walton and McKersie's point in action: value is created by adding issues, not by adding pressure.

6The Ethics Line15 min

Full room. Four tactics drawn from the negotiations you just ran.

The room ranks them from clearly acceptable to clearly unacceptable.

Your task: Defend your ranking. Then answer a fifth question: is using an undisclosed AI assistant during a live negotiation on the acceptable side of the line?

Compare with your Phase 1 answer and note whether your position moved, and why.

7Reflection & Assessment 3 Briefing10 min

Individual writing, then the briefing for Assessment 3, Part 2 (The Pitch).

Your task: Record your reservation point, whether you moved it, and the moment your position felt weakest. Name the framework that would have held it.

In Part 2 your team pitches its project Shark Tank–style to a panel of investors who can approve, reject or counter with tough conditions — a cut budget, added scope, a tighter deadline — which you negotiate live using BATNA, ZOPA and the Harvard Method. The Negotiation Canvas must be submitted.

AI in this unit

What we teach

  • Using AI to prepare: generating the counterparty's likely interests, stress-testing your BATNA estimate, rehearsing objections.
  • The ethics of undisclosed AI in a live negotiation, assessed against categories of acceptable and unacceptable tactics.
  • Why an AI-generated concession plan fails: it cannot read the room, and the timing of concessions is itself a signal.

Practised in session: Phase 3: AI-assisted preparation of the counterparty's interest list, logged. Phases 4–6: no AI at the table.

Rule: AI is permitted and encouraged in negotiation preparation, at Level 2 or 3 with disclosure. AI use during the live pitch and negotiation in Assessment 3 is prohibited and will be treated as an integrity matter.

Debrief questions

  1. How many pairs moved their reservation point in Negotiation 1, and what triggered it?
  2. What joint value did the best Negotiation 2 outcomes create compared with Negotiation 1?
  3. Where did the room place undisclosed AI assistance on the ethical line?
Link to assessmentAssessment 3 — Pitch the Plan, Part 2 (The Pitch): Shark Tank–style pitch and live negotiation of the panel's conditions with BATNA, ZOPA and the Harvard Method. The 5W1H Negotiation Canvas is a required artefact.

After the session, students answer the three reflection questions within 48 hours and add them to their Personal Slibrary.
