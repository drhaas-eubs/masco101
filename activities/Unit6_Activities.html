MASCO101 · Unit 6 · Session Plan

Business Simulation: The NHS National Programme for IT

Seven phases, 150 minutes, no lecture

Business Simulation150 minutes7 phasesSlibraries 11 & 12
Core message · Unit 6

A plan is a hypothesis. A risk register is the honesty test.

Learning focus · ILOs 2 & 3 — Project planning, scheduling & risk. ILO 2: Critically assess the roles and responsibilities of a project manager as part of an organization's strategy. ILO 3: Investigate how to select, develop, plan, schedule and measure projects outcomes and risks.

Learning intention

Students leave able to justify a delivery method against requirement volatility, decompose scope to work-package level under the 100% rule, distinguish formal from effective power in a stakeholder map, and build a scored risk register with named owners and a sustainability (P5) assessment.

Guiding question: Who carries the risk your plan does not mention?

Phase by phase

1Boostcamp Wrap-up15 min

Two episodes were completed before this session: sustainable and socially responsible projects, and risk management in the digital world.

We close both and connect them to the P5 Standard in the gallery.

Your task: Name one risk from the digital-risk episode that your Assessment 3 project carries, and one P5 dimension your project currently ignores.

The dimension most groups ignore is People; the second is Planet. Both are assessable in Assessment 3.

2Gallery Exploration — Slibraries 11 & 1220 min

Ten frameworks: five on project architecture, five on stakeholders, risk and responsibility.

These ten are the working toolkit for Assessment 3.

Your task: In your Assessment 3 group, choose waterfall, agile or hybrid and write one sentence justifying it against requirement stability and feedback speed.

A method chosen because it looks orderly on a chart is not a justification.

3Post-mortem: NHS National Programme for IT30 min

Teams of four. Approximately £10bn over ten years, dismantled in 2011.

The hardest part is separating what was knowable at the time from what is visible only with hindsight.

Your task: Produce a one-page framework diagnosis: which framework, applied properly in 2003, would have surfaced the fatal problem earliest? Mark the decision point where the programme became unrecoverable.

Most groups reach the Power–Interest Grid. Push further: what made clinicians low-power on paper and decisive in practice?

4Build: Scope, WBS and Gantt25 min

In Assessment 3 groups. This is real work on your assessment.

Decompose deliverables, not activities. The 100% rule applies: the WBS captures all of the scope and only the scope.

Your task: Produce a WBS to work-package level and a first Gantt showing dependencies and the critical path. Draw it roughly and early rather than neatly and late.

A Gantt with no dependency links is a list of bars.

— Break · 10 min —

5Stakeholder Mapping & Risk Register30 min

Same groups. Both deliverables are assessed in Assessment 3 Part A.

Every high-scoring risk needs a named owner — a name, not a role.

Your task: (a) Plot every stakeholder on the Power–Interest Grid and mark how each position will shift at your first phase gate; (b) build a register of at least eight risks scored on probability and impact, each with an owner and a response.

Test question: which risk would you be most embarrassed to have omitted if it happened? Add it.

6The Constraint Drop10 min

No preparation. Each group draws three constraint cards, such as: budget cut by 30%; key stakeholder leaves; regulatory deadline moves forward by two months.

Your task: Replan in ten minutes and present the trade-off in Iron Triangle terms, stating explicitly what you give up.

Groups that claim to absorb all three constraints without loss are letting quality absorb them silently.

7Reflection & Assessment 3 Hand-off10 min

Individual writing, then a group action list.

Your task: Write the one risk you are least confident you can manage, and why. Then agree who owns what before the next session.

Part A is submitted in writing. Part B is the live negotiation prepared in Unit 7.

AI in this unit

What we teach

  • Using AI to broaden risk identification: generating candidate risks you did not think of, then filtering them with human judgment about your context.
  • Why AI-generated schedules and estimates produce false confidence: plausible precision on quantities the model cannot know.
  • Sustainable and socially responsible project management: what the P5 dimensions ask that a schedule optimiser never will.

Practised in session: Phase 5: generate candidate risks with AI, then defend or delete each one against your project's actual context. Deletions are logged too.

Rule: AI-generated risks are permitted in Assessment 3 and must be marked as such in the register. Every retained risk needs a human-written justification and a named owner.

Debrief questions

  1. Which framework did most teams pick for the NHS post-mortem, and what did it miss?
  2. How many risk registers had a named person, not a role, against every high risk?
  3. Which Iron Triangle constraint did teams refuse to move in the Constraint Drop?
