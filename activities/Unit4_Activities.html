MASCO101 · Unit 4 · Session Plan

Pitch Arena: The Ninety-Second Room

Seven phases, 150 minutes, no lecture

Pitch Arena150 minutes7 phasesSlibraries 7 & 8
Core message · Unit 4

A brand is not what you claim. It is what survives after you leave the room.

Learning focus · ILO 5 — Personal branding & persuasive delivery. ILO 5: Critically review the communication process, stakeholder communication and communication in diverse work groups.

Learning intention

Students leave able to position a message on the Convince–Clarify spectrum, run Bostelaar's distillation to a single core sentence, structure a pitch answer-first with the Pyramid Principle, and deliver a 90-second pitch to a hard stop and revise it under feedback.

Guiding question: If a model can write your pitch, what is left that only you can say?

Materials and preparation

  • Unit 4 framework gallery (Slibraries 7 & 8)
  • Six printed personal pitches: three from a previous cohort, three generated
  • Bostelaar Distil–Decompose–Prioritise worksheet
  • Pitch scoring rubric (core message, specificity, structure, delivery)
  • Timer with audible 90-second cut-off

Before the session, students read the Unit 4 pre-reading and review Slibraries 7 and 8 in the framework gallery.

Phase by phase

1The Flat Pitch10 min

Six short personal pitches are read aloud. Three were written by students in a previous cohort; three were generated.

No discussion during the reading.

Your task: Identify the three generated pitches and write down the specific tell that gave each away — a tell you can name, not a feeling.

Common tells: no specific number, no named place, no cost paid, and every sentence the same length.

2Gallery Exploration — Slibraries 7 & 820 min

Ten frameworks: five on message, brand and narrative, five on presentation craft.

Bostelaar's Message Strategy Spectrum and Distil–Decompose–Prioritise are the working instruments for the next two phases.

Your task: Place your own current pitch on the Convince–Clarify spectrum and assess it against Bostelaar's criteria: audience skill, awareness, resistance and purpose.

Most student pitches sit too close to Clarify: heavy on what, silent on why. That is a strategy error, not a delivery error.

3Clarify: Distil–Decompose–Prioritise30 min

Individually. Bostelaar's worksheet, run in full and timed.

Do not skip ahead to the one-sentence version.

Your task: (1) Write a full paragraph about what you offer professionally; (2) extract every distinct concept as a separate item; (3) force-rank the concepts; (4) re-summarise in exactly three sentences; (5) compress to exactly one sentence.

The one sentence is your core message. Everything in Phase 4 is built on it.

4Craft: Build the 90-Second Pitch25 min

In pairs; your partner is your first audience and first critic.

Structure: problem → insight → recommendation, with your core message as the recommendation. Answer first, as the Pyramid Principle requires.

Your task: Build a 90-second pitch containing one specific number, one named place or organisation, and one thing that cost you something.

You may use AI as a critic here — ask it to attack your pitch, not to write it — and log what you asked and what you kept.

— Break · 10 min —

5Pitch Arena — Round 130 min

Everyone pitches: ninety seconds, hard stop, standing, no slides.

The room scores clarity of core message, specificity, structure and delivery.

Your task: Deliver, then take feedback on one criterion only: the room names the single highest-leverage change.

You will be cut off at ninety seconds mid-sentence. Executive attention is not extended because you were not finished.

6Pitch Arena — Round 215 min

Volunteers and anyone the room asks for. Revised pitch, no notes.

Your task: Make the single change the room named and deliver again.

The improvement between rounds is usually large and immediate: evidence that presenting is a craft.

7Reflection & Portfolio Entry10 min

Individually, in writing.

Your task: Record your one-sentence core message verbatim and the change you made between rounds. Then answer: what in your pitch could only have been said by you?

If nothing in your pitch is unrepeatable by a model, revise before submission.

AI in this unit

What we teach

  • The tells of AI-generated professional prose: uniform sentence length, absent specifics, no cost paid, and structural convergence across independent authors.
  • Using AI adversarially — asking it to attack a pitch you wrote rather than to write one.
  • Managing digital reputation and online visibility when a model has read everything you have published.

Practised in session: Phase 1 detection exercise; Phase 4 adversarial critique with a prompt log.

Rule: The Assessment 2 pitch must be your own words. AI may be used as a critic and must be disclosed at Level 2. A pitch generated at Level 3 or above will be assessed, but the specificity criterion is where it will lose marks.

Debrief questions

  1. Which tells identified the generated pitches most reliably?
  2. What single change produced the biggest improvement between Round 1 and Round 2?
  3. How many core messages in the room could a competitor also have said?

After the session, students answer the three reflection questions within 48 hours and add them to their Personal Slibrary.
