MASCO101 · Unit 3 · Session Plan

Case Study Duel: Brightfold Retail

Seven phases, 150 minutes, no lecture

Case Study Duel150 minutes7 phasesSlibraries 5 & 6
Core message · Unit 3

AI answers the question you asked. Judgment is knowing it was the wrong question.

Learning focus · ILO 1 — Critical thinking & structured problem-solving. ILO 1: Systematically select key concepts and principles of operations and technology management that need to be implemented to support value creation.

Learning intention

Students leave able to frame a business problem with SCQA before solving it, run an evidenced 5 Whys, name the cognitive bias in their own first reading of a case, and detect the point at which they stop evaluating AI output and start accepting it.

Guiding question: Where does your reasoning end and the model's autocomplete begin?

Materials and preparation

  • Brightfold Retail case (this site), one per pair
  • Sealed prediction envelopes for each team
  • Prompt log template for Pair A (asked / returned / accepted / rejected / verified)
  • One-page analysis template with space for the framework path

Before the session, students read the Unit 3 pre-reading and review Slibraries 5 and 6 in the framework gallery.

Phase by phase

1The Setup10 min

Teams of four, each split into Pair U (unaided) and Pair A (AI-assisted). Assignment is random.

Pair U works with the gallery and the case only. Pair A adds an AI assistant of their choice in Round 2.

Your task: Agree in writing, before you start, what you predict the difference between the pairs will be. Seal it; we open it in Phase 6.

This is a controlled comparison, not a competition. The finding we want is the shape of the difference.

2Gallery Exploration — Slibraries 5 & 620 min

Ten frameworks: five on structured problem-solving, five on judgment and bias.

Both pairs do this phase identically and without AI. Read the First Principles and Design Thinking (Double Diamond) cards closely: they are the course's core tools for reframing a problem before solving it.

Your task: Select the two frameworks your pair will use in Round 1 and write down why. You may not change them after Phase 3 begins.

The IDEAL Model and the 5 Whys are already part of your toolkit. Use them properly, not decoratively.

3Round 1: Unaided Analysis30 min

Both pairs work simultaneously on Brightfold Retail. No AI tools in this phase.

This is the baseline.

Your task: Produce a one-page root-cause analysis and recommendation using your two frameworks: twenty-five minutes of work, five minutes to write the one-sentence recommendation.

Write the recommendation as one sentence with a verb and an owner. If it takes two sentences, you have not decided yet.

4Round 2: AI-Assisted Analysis25 min

Pair A opens AI. Pair U stays unaided and does a second pass with a third framework.

Pair A logs every prompt; the log is part of the deliverable.

Your task: Pair A: use AI to challenge and extend your Round 1 analysis, logging what you asked, what came back, and what you accepted, rejected or verified. Pair U: apply First Principles or the Design Thinking Double Diamond as your third framework and revise.

Pair A: mark in the log the moment you stop reading critically because the output is well written.

— Break · 10 min —

5The Duel25 min

Four teams present. Both pairs from a team present back to back — U first, then A.

Ninety seconds each. The room compares.

Your task: Present your recommendation and the framework path that produced it. Show the reasoning; do not defend it.

The room identifies, for each team, where the two analyses diverged and which divergence mattered.

65 Whys on the Divergence20 min

Open the sealed predictions.

Run an evidenced 5 Whys — not on Brightfold, but on the divergence between your own two analyses.

Your task: Identify the root cause of the most significant difference between Pair U's and Pair A's conclusions: speed, breadth, framing, anchoring, or the quality of the question.

A common finding: the AI-assisted pair produced a broader analysis and a worse question. Test whether that held for you.

7Reflection & Portfolio Entry10 min

Individually, in writing.

Your task: Answer one question honestly: at what specific point in Round 2 did you stop evaluating and start accepting? Cite the prompt log if you have one.

This entry is directly assessable material for the AI reflection component of Assessment 2.

AI in this unit

What we teach

  • Automation bias in analytical work: how fluency of output drives trust independently of accuracy (Parasuraman and Riley, 1997).
  • Prompt logging as a professional record of what was asked, returned, accepted, rejected or verified.
  • The structural asymmetry: models optimise for answering the question asked, which is the one thing that cannot be delegated.

Practised in session: The Duel: the same case worked unaided and AI-assisted, presented side by side, with a 5 Whys run on the divergence.

Rule: Prompt logs are a required deliverable in Phase 4 and an accepted evidence type in Assessment 2. A log that contains no rejected output will be read as an incomplete log.

Debrief questions

  1. Which of the four Brightfold accounts did most pairs accept first, and why?
  2. Did any pair question the definition of the 'channel mix effect' before analysing it?
  3. What did the prompt logs show about when critical reading stopped?
Link to assessmentAssessment 2 — Human Edge Portfolio: prompt log and divergence analysis, the critical AI reflection component.

After the session, students answer the three reflection questions within 48 hours and add them to their Personal Slibrary.
