Role Play — Boardroom: Halden Medical

Seven phases, 150 minutes, no lecture

Role Play — Boardroom150 minutes7 phasesSlibraries 9 & 10
Core message · Unit 5

Executive presence is the ability to stay clear while the pressure rises.

Learning focus · ILO 5 — Business communication & executive presence. ILO 5: Critically review the communication process, stakeholder communication and communication in diverse work groups.

Learning intention

Students leave able to choose between BLUF and narrative openings based on audience and purpose, draft a one-page executive memo that passes the 7 Cs, adapt a message across cultures using the Culture Map and context theory, and hold composure under hostile questioning.

Guiding question: Can you hold a room that does not agree with you?

Materials and preparation

  • Unit 5 framework gallery (Slibraries 9 & 10)
  • Boostcamp episode 'Communication strategies for team success' (completed before class)
  • Six sealed role briefs: Chief Executive, Head of Regulatory Affairs, Head of Manufacturing, Communications Director, Country Manager (Japan), Quality Engineer
  • Observer sheet: who speaks, who is interrupted, when the Quality Engineer stops contributing
  • One-page memo template (BLUF) with a second box for the adapted opening
  • Press-call question cards, including two hostile questions and one based on a leak

Before the session, students read the Unit 5 pre-reading and review Slibraries 9 and 10 in the framework gallery.

Phase by phase

1Boostcamp Wrap-up15 min

You completed the Boostcamp episode 'Communication strategies for team success' before this session. We close it here rather than repeat it.

Three questions to the room, then we move.

Your task: Name one thing from the episode you have already applied, and one you disagreed with. Be specific about the disagreement.

If nobody disagrees with anything, either the episode was trivial or nobody watched it.

2Gallery Exploration — Slibraries 9 & 1020 min

Ten frameworks: five on communication architecture, five on audience, culture and presence.

The Mehrabian card is in the gallery because it is the most misquoted finding in business communication. Read its scope limit.

Your task: In pairs, choose the two frameworks you will use to structure the Scene 2 memo, and identify the cultural scale most likely to cause a problem in this scenario.

BLUF and the Storyline Canvas pull in opposite directions. Choosing between them is the judgment call of the session.

3Role Assignment & Private Briefings15 min

Six roles, each with a private brief containing information the others lack. Some of it conflicts.

Students not in role act as observers.

Your task: Read your brief. Identify your interests, your fallback within the meeting, and the one piece of information you would prefer not to volunteer. Then decide whether to volunteer it.

Do not show your brief to anyone. The information asymmetry is the point.

4Scene 1 — The 8:00 Crisis Meeting25 min

In role, live, twenty minutes. The Chief Executive chairs.

The question: does Halden Medical issue a voluntary recall today, or wait for the second test batch on Friday?

Your task: Reach a real decision as a group within twenty minutes — not a decision to meet again.

Observers track who speaks, who is interrupted, and when the Quality Engineer stops contributing.

— Break · 10 min —

5Scene 2 — Writing the Memo, Live30 min

Pairs across roles. The memo goes to the board, the regulator and, in adapted form, the Japanese distributor.

One decision, three audiences.

Your task: Draft a one-page memo in BLUF structure. Then adapt the opening for the Japanese distributor using the Culture Map and high/low-context analysis. Both versions on one page.

Adaptation is not translation. If the adapted version says the same thing in the same order, you have not adapted it.

6Scene 3 — The 16:00 Press Call25 min

In role. The Communications Director takes questions from the room acting as journalists; twelve minutes, then roles reverse.

Two questions are hostile; one is based on information leaked from Scene 1.

Your task: Communications Director: hold the line without lying. Room: find the gap between what was decided at 08:00 and what is being said at 16:00.

The assessed behaviour is executive presence under pressure: gravitas, not polish.

7Debrief & Reflection10 min

Full group out of role, then individual writing.

Your task: Name the moment the communication actually failed, and the framework that would have caught it.

In most runs the failure is in Scene 1: the person with the raw data stops talking and nobody notices.

AI in this unit

What we teach

  • What AI-generated business prose looks like at sentence level, and how to make yours unmistakably human without making it less professional.
  • Machine translation in high-context settings: why context, hierarchy signalling and sequencing are lost first.
  • AI as a register checker — asking a model where a draft is too blunt or too indirect for a named audience.

Practised in session: Scene 2: draft the memo unaided, then run one AI register check on the adapted version and log what you accepted.

Rule: Live scenes are AI-free. Drafting in Scene 2 may use AI as a checker at Level 2 and must be logged.

Debrief questions

  1. At what minute did the Quality Engineer stop contributing, according to the observers?
  2. Which memos could a board member act on after reading only the first sentence?
  3. What changed — content, order, or register — between the board opening and the distributor opening?

After the session, students answer the three reflection questions within 48 hours and add them to their Personal Slibrary.
