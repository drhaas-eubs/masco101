Brightfold Retail — The 11% That Was Never There

A fashion retailer's margin problem, four plausible explanations, and one definition error

Case studyUnit 3Group work
Core message · Unit 3

AI answers the question you asked. Judgment is knowing it was the wrong question.

Learning focus · ILO 1 — Critical thinking & structured problem-solving. ILO 1: Systematically select key concepts and principles of operations and technology management that need to be implemented to support value creation.

Key frameworks: The IDEAL Model · 5 Whys · The Ladder of Inference · The Cognitive Bias Quartet

Situation

Brightfold Retail operates 84 mid-market fashion stores across Spain, Portugal and southern France, plus an online channel that now accounts for 31% of revenue. In FY2025 the company reported a gross margin of 42.1%, down from 47.3% two years earlier. The board wants the 5.2 points back.

The commercial director attributes the decline to discounting: markdowns as a share of gross sales rose from 18% to 27% over the same period, driven by competitive pressure from two fast-fashion entrants. Her recommendation is a markdown-discipline programme targeting a return to 20%.

Complication

The supply chain director disagrees. Landed cost per unit rose 14% over the period, driven by freight, a currency movement and a supplier consolidation from 61 suppliers to 22. He argues the margin decline is a cost problem wearing a discount costume.

The e-commerce lead offers a third account. Online returns run at 34% against 9% in store, and returns processing costs were reclassified into cost of goods sold in FY2024 during a finance systems migration. Online grew from 12% to 31% of revenue over exactly this period.

The CFO's team has run a regression. It attributes 11% of the margin decline to a 'channel mix effect' that nobody in the room can define, and which appears in the model as a residual. It has been in the board pack for three quarters. No one has asked what it is.

The board meets in nine days. All four explanations are internally consistent and all four are supported by data drawn from the same finance system.

Teaching note and framework pairings (facilitator)

The central teaching point is the definition error. Part of the reported margin decline is an accounting artefact: returns costs moved into COGS in FY2024 without restatement, while the online channel with a 34% returns rate grew rapidly. Strong pairs refuse to analyse the 'channel mix effect' until it is defined, restate margins on a like-for-like basis, and only then weigh discounting against cost. The recommendation should name the CFO as owner of the restatement before any markdown programme is launched.

Suggested pairings: IDEAL + 5 Whys (define before solving, then dig) · Ladder of Inference + Cognitive Bias Quartet (why four smart people reached four answers)
