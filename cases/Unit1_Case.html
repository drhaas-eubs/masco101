MASCO101 · Unit 1 · Case Study

Lumen Analytics — The Intern Who Cited a Case That Never Existed

A mid-sized consultancy discovers a fabricated citation in a delivered client report

Case studyUnit 1Group work
Core message · Unit 1

AI collapses the cost of execution; it raises the price of judgment.

Learning focus · ILO 1 — Foundations & digital fluency. ILO 1: Systematically select key concepts and principles of operations and technology management that need to be implemented to support value creation.

Key frameworks: The Hallucination Risk Ladder · SIFT · The Delegate–Verify–Own Loop

Situation

Lumen Analytics is a 140-person strategy consultancy headquartered in Munich, working mainly with mid-market industrial clients across the DACH region. In February 2026 the firm delivered a market-entry assessment to Vogel Systems, a family-owned components manufacturer considering expansion into Poland.

The report was strong. It ran to forty-two pages, was delivered three days early, and drew on a wider evidence base than the team had managed on comparable engagements. The engagement partner, Marta Reinhardt, signed it off after a two-hour review focused on the recommendation logic and the financial model.

Complication

Eleven days later, Vogel Systems' in-house counsel emailed. He had tried to locate one of the cited sources — a 2024 European Commission working paper on cross-border industrial subsidy rules, quoted twice and used to support the report's central regulatory assumption. The paper did not exist. Neither did its stated authors.

The analyst who wrote the section, Tomás Bauer, had been at the firm for four months. He explained, without evasion, what had happened. He had used an AI assistant to accelerate the regulatory research. It produced a clean, plausible citation with a title, an author list, a document number and a date. He checked the substance of the claim against two other sources and found it broadly consistent. He did not check whether the document itself was real.

Marta's review found something more uncomfortable. Lumen had no verification rule at all. AI use was neither prohibited nor governed; the firm had simply not decided. Three other reports delivered that quarter had been produced under the same conditions. Nobody knew whether they contained the same problem.

Vogel Systems has not terminated the engagement. Their counsel has asked one question, in writing: what will change?

Exhibits

Exhibit 1 — Engagement timeline

  • Kick-off: 12 January 2026
  • Regulatory research with AI assistance: 19–23 January
  • Partner review (2 hours, logic and financial model): 6 February
  • Delivery, three days early: 9 February
  • Client counsel's query: 20 February

Exhibit 2 — Lumen quality controls in place, February 2026

  • Partner sign-off on recommendation logic: yes
  • Financial model peer review: yes
  • Source existence check: no
  • AI use policy or disclosure line: no
  • Reports delivered in Q1 under the same conditions: 4 (including Vogel)

Exhibit 3 — The fabricated reference as it appeared

  • Title, author list, document number and date in correct European Commission house style
  • Quoted twice; supports the report's central regulatory assumption
  • Substance broadly consistent with two genuine sources
Your taskYou are Marta Reinhardt. Prepare a one-page visual argument for the Lumen partner group. Using at least two frameworks from the Unit 1 gallery, address: (a) where in the workflow the failure actually occurred — and it is not where it looks; (b) what verification rule you will impose, and how you will calibrate it so it does not destroy the speed advantage; (c) what you will write back to Vogel Systems' counsel.

Assignment questions

  1. On the Hallucination Risk Ladder, which band does the fabricated reference sit in, and what verification does that band require?
  2. Which SIFT move would have exposed the fabrication in under two minutes?
  3. Where in the Delegate–Verify–Own loop did Lumen's process break, and who owned that step?
  4. Draft the first three sentences of your reply to Vogel Systems' counsel.

Show your framework path on one page, and add your disclosure line from the AI Disclosure Ladder.

Teaching note and framework pairings (facilitator)

Students typically start by blaming Tomás. Redirect them to Exhibit 2: the firm had checks for logic and numbers but none for source existence, so the failure was designed into the process. Strong answers classify citations as Band A (assume fabricated until verified), propose a rule that only Band A and B items require a source-existence check (which protects most of the speed gain), and write to the client with an acknowledgement, the audit of the other three reports, and the new rule.

Suggested pairings: Hallucination Risk Ladder + SIFT (what to check, and how) · Delegate–Verify–Own + AI Disclosure Ladder (who owns the check, and how it is declared)

Used in: Phase 5 of the session plan.
