MASCO101 · Unit 2 · Case Study

Two CVs, One Role — The Shortlist That Rewarded the Wrong Signal

An AI-assisted screening process, two candidates, and a definition of merit nobody wrote down

Case studyUnit 2Group work
Core message · Unit 2

A self-assessment you cannot act on is a horoscope.

Learning focus · ILO 1 — Self-assessment & personal positioning. ILO 1: Systematically select key concepts and principles of operations and technology management that need to be implemented to support value creation.

Key frameworks: Personal SWOT/TOWS · The Johari Window · The Big Five · The Cognitive Bias Quartet

Situation

Aurelis Group is a European logistics operator with 3,000 employees. In March 2026 it opened a single graduate position in its strategy function and received 1,140 applications. The talent team used an AI screening tool to reduce the pool to sixty, then a human panel to reduce sixty to six.

Two candidates reached the final two. Candidate A had a first-class degree from a well-known school, three internships at recognisable firms, and a CV written in confident, keyword-dense professional English. The screening tool ranked her fourth of 1,140.

Complication

Candidate B had a good but not outstanding degree from a less known institution, one internship, and eighteen months running operations for a family freight business during a period when it nearly failed and then did not. His CV was plainly written and understated. The screening tool ranked him 812th. He reached the final six only because a panel member pulled twenty rejected CVs at random as a spot check.

In the final interview the panel split. Two members backed Candidate A: stronger credentials, more polish, lower risk. Two backed Candidate B: he had made real decisions under real constraint, and he described a mistake he had made and what it cost, without being asked.

The Head of Strategy, who holds the casting vote, asked the talent team one question: what was the screening tool actually optimising for? Once someone checked, the answer was similarity to the CVs of people already hired into the function over the previous four years.

Exhibits

Exhibit 1 — The funnel

  • Human panel shortlist: 6
  • Final: 2
  • Random spot-check of rejected CVs: 20, of which 1 reached the final six

Exhibit 2 — Candidate evidence

  • Candidate A: first-class degree; 3 internships; tool rank 4; interview: polished, low-risk answers

Exhibit 3 — Role profile (strategy function)

  • Structure ambiguous problems for senior management
  • Work across operations and finance
  • Make recommendations under time and data constraints
Your taskYou are the Head of Strategy. Using at least two frameworks from the Unit 2 gallery, prepare a visual argument answering: (a) what signal the screening process actually rewarded, and what signal the role required; (b) which candidate has the stronger evidenced profile, and on what criteria; (c) what you would change in the process, given that you cannot read 1,140 CVs by hand.

Assignment questions

  1. Build an evidenced SWOT for each candidate. Which cells are evidenced and which are inferred from polish?
  2. Which of the four biases in the Cognitive Bias Quartet shaped the panel split?
  3. What did Candidate B's unprompted mistake reveal in Johari terms?
  4. Design one structural change to the screening process that costs less than a second full review.

Show your framework path on one page, and add your disclosure line from the AI Disclosure Ladder.

Teaching note and framework pairings (facilitator)

Groups often split along the same line as the panel. Push them to separate evidence of performance under constraint from signals of polish and prestige. The key insight is Exhibit 3: the role asks for decisions under constraint, and only Candidate B has evidence of that. Strong process fixes include writing the success criteria before screening, auditing the tool's training reference set, and keeping a random-sample review of rejected applications as a standing control.

Suggested pairings: Personal SWOT/TOWS + Big Five (evidence versus claim) · Cognitive Bias Quartet + Johari Window (how the panel and the tool saw differently)
