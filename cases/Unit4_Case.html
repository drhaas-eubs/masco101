The Ninety-Second Room — Three Candidates, One Slot

An investment committee, a shortlist, and the pitch nobody remembered

Case studyUnit 4Group work
Core message · Unit 4

A brand is not what you claim. It is what survives after you leave the room.

Learning focus · ILO 5 — Personal branding & persuasive delivery. ILO 5: Critically review the communication process, stakeholder communication and communication in diverse work groups.

Key frameworks: The Message Strategy Spectrum · Distil–Decompose–Prioritise · The Golden Circle · The Pyramid Principle

Situation

Kesterling Partners runs a graduate accelerator that takes four people a year from roughly two thousand applications. The final stage is a ninety-second pitch to a committee of six, delivered standing, without slides. The committee has done this eleven times and has never extended the time limit.

In June 2026, three candidates reached the final round for one remaining slot. On paper, all three were comparably qualified.

Complication

Candidate One opened with her background: degree, institution, two internships, technical skills, languages. At sixty seconds she reached what she wanted to do with the accelerator. She was cut off at ninety, four words into her final sentence. Two committee members later described the pitch as 'solid'. None could recall the final sentence.

Candidate Two opened with a claim: that mid-market European manufacturers systematically misprice their own transition risk, and that he had watched it happen from inside one. He gave one number — a €400,000 contract his father's firm lost because nobody had modelled a regulatory change that had been public for two years — and connected it to what he wanted to build. He finished at eighty-four seconds.

Candidate Three delivered the most polished ninety seconds of the day: fluent, well structured, no filler, strong close. In the debrief, one committee member could not name a single specific thing she had said. Another found the pitch structure almost identical, clause for clause, to two other pitches that morning. All three had plausibly used the same tool to prepare.

The committee gave the slot to Candidate Two. The chair recorded the reason in one line: he was the only one who told us something that cost him something.

Teaching note and framework pairings (facilitator)

Candidate One built the pitch bottom-up (what, then why), so the core message arrived too late — a Pyramid Principle and Message Strategy error. Candidate Three shows structural convergence: a fluent pitch without specifics is indistinguishable from others built with the same tool. Candidate Two led with a claim (why), supported it with a costly, specific fact, and finished early. The AI implication is that AI can critique structure and timing but cannot supply the lived specifics that make a pitch memorable.

Suggested pairings: Message Strategy Spectrum + Pyramid Principle (what goes first) · Distil–Decompose–Prioritise + Golden Circle (what the one sentence is, and why it matters)
