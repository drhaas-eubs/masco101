Key framework: The Johari Window

The argument

There is a category of information about you that no amount of reflection will produce, no personality instrument will detect, and no AI system will infer: what other people observe about how you work that you have never been told.

Luft and Ingham called it the blind pane. It is the one quadrant of the Johari Window that is closed to introspection by definition — known to others, unknown to you. The only mechanism that opens it is feedback, given specifically and received without defence. Both halves are difficult, which is why the pane often stays shut for entire careers.

This matters more now, not less. A model can read every word you have written and produce a fluent profile of your interests, your register and your likely working style. It cannot tell you that you interrupt people when you are anxious, that your team has stopped bringing you bad news, or that seniors see you as decisive while juniors see you as unapproachable. Those facts exist only in other people's heads, and they frequently determine careers.

The key framework

The Johari Window

The Johari Window maps what is known and unknown to you against what is known and unknown to others. The open pane is shared knowledge. The blind pane is visible to others but not to you. The hidden pane is what you know and withhold. The unknown pane is latent and surfaces in new situations. Feedback shrinks the blind pane; deliberate disclosure shrinks the hidden pane.

Open — known to self and others
Blind — known to others, not to you
Hidden — known to you, withheld from others
Unknown — surfaced only by new situations

This unit therefore runs two tracks. The instruments track — SWOT, Big Five, DISC, MBTI — gives you structured vocabulary and teaches you to read the evidence base behind an instrument before you trust it. The Big Five has strong empirical support and describes traits as continuous dimensions. MBTI is widely used but sorts people into forced categories and has weak test–retest reliability; it is useful as a conversation language, not as a selection tool.

The feedback track gives you what the instruments cannot. And the planning track closes the loop: a diagnosis only becomes useful when it produces a dated action. Weihrich's TOWS matrix turns the four SWOT cells into four strategies, and a SMART objective turns a strategy into something another person could verify.

A worked example

A graduate believes her strength is thoroughness. In a structured feedback round, two teammates independently say that her updates arrive late because she waits until everything is perfect. The observation moves from her blind pane to her open pane. Her SWOT changes: thoroughness stays a strength, but a new weakness appears — late visibility of work in progress. Her TOWS action (WO) becomes: share a draft at the 50% point on every group deliverable this term, verified by her team lead.

Three takeaways
  • The blind pane is inaccessible to introspection and to AI profiling alike.
  • Instruments differ enormously in evidential quality; check before you trust.
  • A self-assessment that does not produce a dated action is not a diagnostic.
Two questions to bring to the session
  1. Which strength on your CV could you evidence with a specific date, audience and outcome?
  2. Who has seen you work under pressure and would tell you something uncomfortable if you asked?
