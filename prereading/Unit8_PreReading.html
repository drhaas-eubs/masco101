Key framework: Energy Management and the Delegate–Verify–Own Loop

The argument

Time management often fails for a structural reason: time is fixed and non-renewable, so every system built on it is ultimately a system for squeezing. Loehr and Schwartz reframed the problem. The binding constraint on professional performance is energy, and energy, unlike time, is renewable and responds to how you cycle effort and recovery.

They describe four dimensions: physical, emotional, mental and spiritual. The physical dimension is the base; the other three degrade quickly without it. For students the implication is uncomfortable: sleep is not a trade-off you can make for productivity. It is the substrate everything else runs on.

Covey's urgent/important matrix does the prioritisation work. Its lasting insight is that important but non-urgent work is the only quadrant that compounds, and it is always the one sacrificed because nothing in it is shouting. Your development, your network, your Slibrary and your portfolio all sit there. Personal OKRs protect it: one qualitative objective and three measurable key results per term.

The key framework

Energy Management and the Delegate–Verify–Own Loop

The Task Delegation Matrix plots the consequence of an error against how easily the output can be checked. Low stakes, easy to verify: delegate freely. Low stakes, hard to verify: delegate and sample-check. High stakes, easy to verify: delegate and check every time. High stakes, hard to verify: keep the task.

Then the AI layer. Delegating to a model is easy; delegating well is not. The Task Delegation Matrix asks two questions: what does an error cost, and how cheaply can the output be checked? Low stakes and easy to verify: delegate freely. High stakes and hard to verify: do not delegate. The dangerous quadrant is high stakes and easy to verify, because verification is possible and cheap — and quietly stops once the output has been right several times in a row.

The Delegate–Verify–Own loop names the discipline. Delegation is explicit and bounded. Verification is proportionate to consequence, not to effort saved. Accountability never transfers: you signed it, so you answer for it. Attribution completes the loop. A model cannot be an author because it cannot take responsibility, so any substantive AI contribution to academic or professional work is disclosed, and the factual claims remain yours to verify.

A worked example

A student uses AI for three tasks. Reformatting her bibliography is low stakes and easy to check: she delegates freely. Summarising twenty articles for background reading is low stakes but hard to verify: she delegates and reads three of the sources in full as a sample. The statistics in her assessed report are high stakes and easy to verify: AI may draft the table, but she checks every number against the source and records the check. The final recommendation of her report is high stakes and hard to verify: she writes it herself and declares her AI use at the correct level.

Three takeaways
  • Energy is renewable and multidimensional; time is neither. Manage the renewable one.
  • Important but non-urgent work is the only quadrant that compounds.
  • Verification is calibrated to consequence, not to how much effort it saved.
Two questions to bring to the session
  1. Last week, where did your time go, and where did your energy go? Were they the same?
  2. Which recurring task would you delegate to AI, and which quadrant of the matrix does it fall into?
