Clarify Before You Craft

Read before Unit 4 · Key framework: Bostelaar's Message Strategy Spectrum

Core message · Unit 4

A brand is not what you claim. It is what survives after you leave the room.

Learning focus · ILO 5 — Personal branding & persuasive delivery. ILO 5: Critically review the communication process, stakeholder communication and communication in diverse work groups.

Key framework: Bostelaar's Message Strategy Spectrum

The argument

Kurt Bostelaar's playbook opens with a separation that sounds obvious and is rarely observed: clarify the message first, craft the story second. Most people do both at once, discovering what they think while already trying to make it persuasive. The result communicates something, but rarely what was intended.

The clarifying work has a method. Write a full paragraph about what you mean, without editing. Decompose it into its component concepts, listed separately. Force-rank those concepts — rank, not group, because the discipline is in being made to choose. Re-summarise in three sentences, then in one. That single sentence is your core message, and it has been arrived at rather than asserted.

Almost every student pitch sits too far toward the informational end of the spectrum. It lists what you have done, in order and competently, to an audience that has not yet been given a reason to care. No amount of delivery coaching fixes that; it is a message-strategy error.

The key framework

Bostelaar's Message Strategy Spectrum

The Message Strategy Spectrum positions any message between Convince and Clarify. Convince messages are motivational: they answer why something matters and build trust. Clarify messages are informational: they unpack what something is. Most real messages blend both. The right position is diagnosed from the audience's skill, awareness and likely resistance, and from your purpose: low awareness and high resistance push you towards Convince; high skill and an instructional purpose push you towards Clarify.

Convince — motivational, answers why it matters
Clarify — informational, answers what it is
Assess audience skill, awareness, resistance and purpose
Low awareness and high resistance push towards Convince

Structure then decides whether the message survives time pressure. Minto's Pyramid Principle puts the answer first and supports it with a small number of grouped arguments. In a ninety-second slot, a chronological structure almost guarantees that the most important sentence is the one that gets cut.

The AI dimension is sharp. A model can produce a fluent, well-structured pitch in seconds, and in a recruitment round several candidates may arrive with pitches that are structurally identical because they were built the same way. What survives that convergence is specificity: a number, a place, a thing that cost you something. Personal branding follows the same logic. A brand is not what you claim about yourself; it is the sentence other people can repeat after you have left. The Personal Brand Pyramid builds that sentence from the bottom up — attributes, benefits, values, personality, and finally a one-line essence — and your LinkedIn profile is where most people first meet it. After this session you audit your profile against the pyramid and rewrite your professional narrative.

A worked example

A student applying to a sustainability consultancy drafts: 'I studied economics, interned at two banks, and speak four languages.' The audience (a recruiter) has low awareness of her and moderate resistance (hundreds of similar applicants), so the pitch needs more Convince. After distillation, her core sentence becomes: 'I help finance teams price climate risk they currently ignore.' She supports it with one number from her internship — a €2m loan she flagged for flood exposure — and one named place. The pitch now starts with why and ends with a request.

Three takeaways
  • Clarify the message before crafting the story; doing both at once corrupts both.
  • Distil–Decompose–Prioritise ends in one sentence, arrived at rather than asserted.
  • Position on the Convince–Clarify spectrum is a diagnosis, not a preference.
Two questions to bring to the session
  1. Where does your current LinkedIn headline sit on the Convince–Clarify spectrum, and is that where your audience needs it?
  2. What is one specific number from your own experience that a competitor could not claim?
